        **ARIA** is a research platform for evaluating whether information systems and applications continue to function as intended under disaster-related disruptions.

        By reproducing disasters, infrastructure failures, information services, and human behavior in an integrated environment, ARIA enables problems to be identified before real disasters occur.

  - block: markdown
    id: about
    content:
      title: 'About ARIA'
      subtitle: ''
      text: |-
        Modern disaster response depends on many interconnected IT systems. Even when each individual component works correctly, failures in communications, power, external services, or the surrounding environment can prevent information from reaching the people who need it.

        ARIA provides an experimental environment in which these dependencies can be reproduced and observed. It combines disaster simulation, infrastructure conditions, information services, applications, and human behavior to examine **where a system works, where it fails, and why**.

      title: 'Research Themes'
      subtitle: ''
      text: |-
        ### 1. Development of the Integration Platform
        We develop the ARIA platform and methods for integrating heterogeneous simulators, infrastructure models, services, and applications into a common experimental environment.

        ### 2. Analysis Using the Integrated Platform
        We use ARIA to investigate the effects of disaster-related disruptions on information delivery, application behavior, evacuation support, and other disaster-response services, including how failures propagate across dependent systems.

        ### 3. Synthetic Disaster Data
        We develop methods for generating disaster scenarios and synthetic disaster data that can be used as experimental conditions, including rainfall and flood scenarios and future extensions to other hazards.
